# Decision log

| 2026-11-17 | Client schedules are learned from past bookings and confirmed by the coordinator, and kept in the settings file (`client_schedules`, settings version 2); a week can be drafted from them before CareLineLive generates its bookings, and the real bookings are then checked against the draft. Carers with no team in CareLineLive or the settings file are not planned (they used to be placed in the busiest region). Dorchester is planned together with Weymouth (regions "Leeds" and "Weymouth"; "Dorset" is read as "Weymouth") | CareLineLive exposes no client schedule entries through its API or reports, so the only source is the bookings it has already generated. Trade-off: early drafts (cover can be arranged a week ahead) against schedules drifting from CareLineLive; mitigated by flagging clients whose schedule changed after the weeks learned from, by leaving unconfirmed, suspended and schedule-less clients out with a warning, and by the final check of the real week against the draft (added, removed, retimed). Not planning no-team carers avoids guessing a region for them: their bookings go to other carers and a warning names them, at the cost of leaving a carer out until their team is set. Planning Dorchester with Weymouth matches how the agency works; the cost is that the two towns share one set of carers and one equal-hours target | Decided |
| 2026-11-17 | Travel times and distances come from local OSRM road routing on public OpenStreetMap extracts (Dorset and West Yorkshire, car profile, one container per county on 127.0.0.1), with the old straight-line estimate as the fallback (off, unreachable, places outside the map, walking and bus carers). The same figures score the current rota and the plan | The estimate over- or under-states real drives and the coordinator judges the plan on travel. Trade-off: road accuracy, and no addresses or coordinates leave the machine (unlike Google Maps), versus Docker and about 80 MB of maps to keep running and updated, no live traffic, and carers or clients outside the two extracts falling back to estimates (with a warning) | Decided |
| 2026-11-17 | Carers are available at any time of day by default (00:00 to 24:00, one block a day, minus approved leave); the coordinator restricts a carer only where needed by entering working hours, and the engine decides who works when ("we just allocate the working hours"). A carer with no hours entered is therefore planned, not skipped; a carer is left out only if marked not available (`available_any_time: false`) or not active. The weekly maximum and equal-hours balancing still apply (user, 2026-11-17) | Removes the biggest data-entry job (every carer's hours) before the test and fits the user's view that the engine should shape the roster. Trade-off: no input needed, versus the engine possibly planning very long or split days (for example a 06:00 visit and a 23:00 visit for one carer): the engine has no per-day hours limit, span limit or break rule, only the weekly maximum and a rest after anything running past midnight, until per-day limits exist (proposed: a max hours per day and a max span setting) | Decided |
| 2026-11-17 | Improve my rota: the change cost defaults to **£10** (1000p per carer change), with **£3 / £5 / £10 / £20** offered on the Run screen. A new **arrival tolerance** setting (`arrival_tolerance_minutes`, default placeholder **15 min, to confirm with the coordinator and Loidis's contracts**) makes a start-time shift within it, with the carer unchanged, a note instead of a change to apply. Reporting only: the solver is unchanged | At £10 the trial gave about 395 carer changes in a week, a size a coordinator can review; lower costs give more changes for a smaller saving, £20 keeps more of the current rota. Most time shifts come from the engine's travel model, not a real saving, so asking the coordinator to apply each would add work for no gain. Trade-off: a tolerance hides small shifts, so the real arrival time may drift from the booked one by up to the tolerance (a client may be seen up to 15 min off the booked time); it is a placeholder, and no regulatory or contractual standard is claimed. Because the solver ignores it, shifts are still planned with travel that fits the model | Decided (15 min value open) |
| 2026-11-17 | The first version has a clean look that is familiar to a CareLineLive user: the same words (bookings, rounds, roster, unallocated, booking types, teams), a carer-per-row day grid like their roster, familiar status colours (never colour alone) and icons from a standard open icon set (for example Lucide) chosen to mean the same things. We do not copy CareLineLive's logo, icons, colours or screen designs | The coordinator uses CareLineLive all day, so familiar words and layout cut training and mistakes. Trade-off: some similarity to their roster is deliberate, but copying their branding or designs would use their product and could cause confusion or a dispute, so ours is a design of its own | Decided |
| 2026-11-17 | The engine test is hosted on our own hosting, with must-haves: Loidis's written agreement and data processing agreement before real data (`COMPLIANCE.md` item 3), UK region, HTTPS, a simple login for the coordinator and dev team only, no third-party scripts, analytics, fonts or map tiles in the page, no personal data in logs, and the CareLineLive token only on the server, from a view-only CareLineLive user (not yet set up). This is not legal advice | Replaces the earlier option of one Loidis computer: the coordinator can use it from anywhere and we can fix problems quickly. Trade-off: real data travels to a server we run, so the agreement, UK hosting and login are conditions of starting, versus a Loidis machine where data never leaves the agency but set-up and support are harder. Keeping the server stateless (next entry) limits what is exposed | Decided |
| 2026-11-17 | The engine test has two modes. **New rota (from scratch):** the engine allocates every booking; output is the full allocation per carer per day. **Improve my rota:** the engine starts from the coordinator's current allocation (from the bookings CSV) and changes a visit only when the gain is worth a change (a per-change cost setting); output is a short list of changes, each with what to change (booking ID, client reference, date, time, from carer(s), to carer(s), new time) and why (travel saved, familiar carer, fills an unallocated visit, evens hours). Both show the same overview against the current rota. The coordinator applies the result in CareLineLive by hand | The trial moved 626 visits to get its gains, which is too much work and disruption to apply by hand each week; an improve mode gives the coordinator a handful of worthwhile changes, while the from-scratch mode shows what the engine can do unconstrained. Trade-off: two modes to build, explain and test, and the per-change cost is a new setting that needs tuning on real weeks | Decided |
| 2026-11-17 | No database for the engine test. Nothing about clients, carers or rotas is stored on the server: each run is upload, run, review, download; uploaded files and results are deleted after the run; no personal data in server logs. The settings the coordinator enters (working hours, weekly maximum, skills, DBS/training dates, do-not-send, safeguarding bars, gender preferences, required skills, time-critical visits, carer name matches) are downloaded as a settings file the coordinator keeps and uploads with the next week. Stored history and "find last week's run" move to later; PostgreSQL stays the choice once the engine is proven | Least personal data held, and no storage, backups, retention or audit-log work before we know the engine is worth it. Trade-off: the settings file can be lost, go stale or be copied around (it holds personal data, so Loidis must keep it carefully), the coordinator has more to manage by hand, and there is no history of runs or continuity until the database exists | Decided |
| 2026-11-17 | MVP technology: Next.js for the screens, the rota engine stays in Python (OR-Tools) as a separate service the web app calls, PostgreSQL for the app's data; the CareLineLive import runs on the Python side | Keeps the architecture in `PLAN.md` and the engine and import code that already work. Trade-off: two languages and three parts to run and package, versus a single Python app that would be quicker to start but likely rebuilt later | Decided |
| 2026-11-17 | The rota companion MVP ([`MVP.md`](MVP.md)) is built for Loidis only: no multi-agency support (no agency IDs, no per-agency settings or importers). | 2026-11-17 | The shadow trial's setting for the coordinator's review is a continuity cost of £10 per visit by a carer unfamiliar to the client (`continuity_penalty_pence` 1000), measured against the previous 4 weeks. Other trial settings: visit window ±30 min, move cost 15p per minute, day 06:30-22:30, weekly maximum 60 h. Full runs and results: [`SHADOW_TRIAL.md`](SHADOW_TRIAL.md) | At the default cost (60p) the engine's continuity was below the coordinator's actual week (0.277 vs 0.349 overall; Leeds 0.354 vs 0.555). At £10 it was higher (0.566 after the 2026-11-17 review fixes; Leeds 0.701 vs 0.555; Dorset 0.487 vs 0.231) with travel time 16% lower and miles 20% lower than the actual week, but 3 fewer visits fully staffed (1,061 vs 1,064) and 626 visits moved up to 30 min. The trial applied no skills, gender, do-not-send, safeguarding, DBS or training checks. Trade-off: about 12 h more travel and 200 more miles than at the default cost, and less even hours (stdev 7.2 h vs 5.8 h at the default, earlier run before the fixes; 7.9 h after them), for much better continuity. Continuity is still a tie-breaker in principle, but the trial showed a low cost lets the engine break existing carer-client relationships. One week only, so to be revisited after the coordinator's review | Decided |

| 2026-11-16 | The engine's hours goal is to balance carers' hours equally within each team (Leeds; Dorchester/Weymouth). It is a weighted preference, not a hard rule (travel and continuity still count, so it will not add long trips just to move an hour); a per-carer weekly maximum is the hard limit. Carers who cover several teams can even things out across them. Rest and working-time limits stay as engine rules and must be confirmed with Loidis's HR adviser before being relied on | Loidis contracts do not record hours we can read, and 8 carers are "paid hours only", so a contracted share (the engine's current wording) cannot be calculated. Trade-off: equal hours vs contracted share (equal hours ignore that some carers want fewer hours, which the weekly maximum partly covers), and fairness vs travel (a heavier balance weight means more miles) | Decided |
